#### AI Workout/Diet Plan Generation — Capabilities and Limitations

**What AI can do**:
- Generate personalized workout plans in seconds based on goals, fitness level, equipment, limitations
- Create meal plans with caloric targets, macro splits, recipes, portion sizes, grocery lists
- Adjust sets, reps, and weight based on logged performance and recovery (Fitbod)
- Provide first drafts for trainer review

**Evidence on accuracy**:
- **American Heart Association** (January 2026): AI-generated exercise recommendations were **~90% accurate** in matching established facts but only **~40% comprehensive**.
- **Dergaa et al. (2023)**: AI-generated exercise prescriptions "lacked precision in addressing individual health conditions and goals, often prioritizing excessive safety over effectiveness."
- **Puce et al. (2025)**: Generative AI programs "generally adhere to established exercise guidelines but often lack specificity."
- Macro estimates in AI diet plans: **within 10-15% of actual needs** but can be off for edge cases.

**Key limitations**:
1. Cannot build accountability (only 10% of consumers prefer AI to human coach)
2. Cannot read the room (energy, mood, body language)
3. Outputs need human judgment — every AI workout/meal plan is a draft, not a deliverable
4. Cannot replace trust or emotional connection

### B1. WhatsApp Business Platform Pricing in India (2026)

#### Per-Message Pricing (Effective 1 January 2026)

Meta moved from conversation-based pricing to **per-message pricing** on 1 July 2025. Current India rates:

| Category | Rate (INR) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing** | **₹1.09** per message | All marketing templates charged |
| **Utility** | **₹0.145** per message | Free within 24-hour CSW |
| **Authentication** | **₹0.145** per message | OTP, account verification |
| **Service** | **FREE** | User-initiated; unlimited free service messages |

**Key pricing dynamics:**
- **Non-template messages** sent within an open 24-hour Customer Service Window (CSW) are **free**
- **Utility templates** delivered within an open CSW are **free**
- **Free Entry Point (FEP) window**: If a user messages you via a Click-to-WhatsApp Ad or Facebook Page CTA, and you respond within 24 hours, a 72-hour FEP window opens — all messages free during this period
- **Volume tiers**: Utility and authentication rates can decrease based on monthly message volume, aggregated at the business portfolio level across all WABAs. Tiers reset monthly.
- **Pricing calendar**: Meta may update pricing up to 4 times per year (Jan 1, Apr 1, Jul 1, Oct 1), with 1-6 months advance notice.

**2025-2026 Pricing Changes:**
- **1 Jul 2025**: Switched from conversation-based to per-message pricing; service conversations became free
- **1 Jan 2026**: India marketing rate increased to ₹1.09; billing localization launched for India (INR billing)
- **1 Apr 2026**: India authentication-international rate increased
- **1 Jul 2026**: India moved to standalone rate card (no longer regional "Rest of" pricing)

#### Service Conversation Rules (24-Hour Service Window)

- When a WhatsApp user sends you a message, a **24-hour Customer Service Window (CSW)** opens
- Each new message from the user **resets** the 24-hour timer
- During the CSW, you can send **any type of message** (free-form text, images, etc.) at no charge
- Utility templates sent within the CSW are also free
- After the CSW closes, you can only send **pre-approved template messages** (which are charged)
- Service conversations (user-initiated) are **always free** regardless of volume

#### Template Approval Process

- Templates must be submitted for approval through the WhatsApp Business Management API or Meta Business Suite
- Templates are categorized as **Marketing**, **Utility**, or **Authentication** — the category determines the per-message rate
- **Approval timeline**: Typically 24-48 hours; can take longer during peak periods
- Templates must include the **brand name** and follow WhatsApp's content guidelines
- **Variable fields** use `{{1}}`, `{{2}}` syntax
- Rejected templates can be resubmitted after fixing issues

#### Opt-In Rules for Marketing Messages

- **Explicit opt-in required** before sending marketing messages to users
- Opt-in must be **freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous**
- Pre-ticked boxes or implied consent are **not valid**
- Businesses must **retain records** of opt-in consent
- Users can opt out at any time; honor opt-out requests immediately
- Marketing messages can only be sent to users who have opted in AND within the template messaging framework

### B2. UPI AutoPay / e-Mandate Rules

#### RBI Digital Payments E-Mandate Framework, 2026

On **21 April 2026**, the RBI issued the **Digital Payments E-Mandate Framework, 2026**, consolidating eight earlier circulars (2019-2024) into a single unified rulebook. Effective immediately.

| Rule | Details |
|---|---|
| **AFA for recurring debits** | **No AFA required** for recurring transactions up to **₹15,000** per transaction |
| **Higher threshold** | **₹1,00,000** no-AFA limit for insurance premiums, mutual fund SIPs, and credit card bill payments |
| **AFA above threshold** | Transactions above ₹15,000 (or ₹1 lakh for eligible categories) require AFA each cycle |
| **First transaction** | Always requires AFA (mandate registration) |
| **Pre-debit notification** | **Mandatory 24 hours before** every debit |
| **Post-debit notification** | **Mandatory** after every successful debit |
| **Customer charges** | **Zero charges** to customers for e-mandate facility |
| **Card reissuance** | Existing mandates **automatically map** to reissued cards |
| **Variable mandates** | Customers can set **maximum transaction value** cap |
| **Modification/Withdrawal** | Requires AFA validation |

#### AFA (Additional Factor Authentication) Threshold

- **Previous limit**: ₹5,000 (pre-2022)
- **Current limit (2026)**: **₹15,000** per transaction for general recurring payments
- **Enhanced limit**: **₹1,00,000** for insurance premiums, mutual fund SIPs, credit card bill payments
- AFA factors: something you know (PIN/password), something you have (phone/token), something you are (biometrics)
- RBI is encouraging alternatives to SMS OTPs: in-app authentication, biometrics, device binding, cryptographic tokens

#### Pre-Debit Notification Requirements

- Must be sent **at least 24 hours before** every recurring debit
- Must include: merchant name, transaction amount, date/time of debit, mandate reference number, reason for debit, **opt-out facility**
- If customer opts out, the specific debit is blocked; mandate remains active for future cycles

#### Post-debit Notification Requirements

- Must include: merchant name, amount debited, date/time of debit, transaction reference, mandate reference, reason for debit, **grievance redressal details** (new in 2026 framework)

#### UPI AutoPay Limits

| Limit Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| **Standard per-transaction cap** | **₹15,000** |
| **Insurance premiums** | **₹1,00,000** |
| **Mutual fund SIPs** | **₹1,00,000** |
| **Credit card bill payments** | **₹1,00,000** |
| **Monthly aggregate** | No explicit monthly cap published |

- UPI AutoPay was launched by NPCI on **22 July 2020**
- Over **120 million** UPI AutoPay mandates created monthly in India (as of 2026)
- Over **3 million** new mandates created monthly
- Supported frequencies: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly

#### Payment Subscription APIs

| Provider | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Razorpay Subscriptions** | UPI Autopay, card e-mandates, eNACH; smart routing; configurable retry logic; webhooks for mandate lifecycle; pre-debit notification workflow support |

1. Use **Razorpay Subscriptions** or **Cashfree Subscriptions** as the payment layer
2. Default to **UPI AutoPay** for memberships under ₹15,000/month
3. Offer **card e-mandate** for premium memberships above ₹15,000
4. Implement **hybrid approach** for maximum reach
5. Send pre-debit notifications via **WhatsApp** (utility template) + **SMS** (DLT-registered)

#### SAC Code for Gym Services

- **SAC Code 999722**: Recreational and sporting services including fitness centers
- **HSN Code 9506**: Applies to gym equipment — taxed at 18%

#### Composition Scheme

- **NOT available** for gym services at the 5% rate
- The 5% rate without ITC is a **special rate** under the GST rate schedule, not the composition scheme
- Gym businesses must register as **normal taxpayers** if turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states)
- **Voluntary registration** recommended even below threshold for credibility and corporate tie-ups

#### ITC Considerations

- **ITC is NOT available** on the 5% GST rate for gym services
- GST paid on equipment (18%), rent (18%), maintenance, supplements, etc. **cannot be claimed as credit**
- This significantly increases the **effective cost** for gym owners
- **Before Sept 2025**: 18% GST with ITC — lower effective cost despite higher rate
- **After Sept 2025**: 5% GST without ITC — higher effective cost despite lower rate

**Practical impact example:**
- Old regime: ₹10,000 membership + 18% GST = ₹11,800; ITC on expenses reduces net cost
- New regime: ₹10,000 membership + 5% GST = ₹10,500; no ITC on expenses; net cost higher

### B4. DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025

#### Overview

- **DPDP Act 2023** (No. 22 of 2023): Received Presidential assent on **11 August 2023**
- **DPDP Rules 2025**: Notified on **13 November 2025** via Gazette G.S.R. 846(E)
- **Data Protection Board of India**: Established 13 November 2025 (G.S.R. 844(E)) in NCT of Delhi
- **Compliance deadline**: **13 May 2027** (18 months from Rules notification); MeitY proposed accelerating to **November 2026** (12 months) — not yet formally confirmed

#### Key Obligations for Data Fiduciaries (Gym SaaS = Data Fiduciary)

1. **Obtain valid consent** before collecting personal data (Section 5)
2. **Provide notice** in clear, plain language before collection (Rule 3)
3. **Implement reasonable security safeguards** (Section 8(5), Rule 6)
4. **Notify Data Protection Board** of personal data breaches (Rule 7)
5. **Notify affected Data Principals** within 72 hours of breach (Rule 7)
6. **Erase personal data** when purpose is fulfilled or consent withdrawn (Section 8(7), Rule 8)
7. **Appoint a grievance redressal mechanism** (Section 13)
8. **Respond to Data Principal requests** within specified timelines (Rule 14)
9. **Maintain audit trails** — access and activity logs retained for **one year** (Rule 6)
10. **Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments** (if designated as SDF)

#### Consent Requirements

| Requirement | Description |
|---|---|
| **Free** | Not bundled or coerced; no denial of service for refusal |
| **Specific** | For a defined purpose; not blanket consent |
| **Informed** | Supported by a clear notice explaining what data, why, and rights |
| **Unconditional** | No pre-ticked boxes or deceptive design |
| **Unambiguous** | Clear affirmative action required |

**Notice requirements:**
- Must be in clear, plain language
- Must state: personal data to be collected, purpose of processing, how to exercise rights
- Must be available in **22 languages** (Eighth Schedule of Constitution) if requested
- **Dark patterns** are prohibited

**Legitimate uses (Section 7)** — consent NOT required for:
- Employment purposes
- Medical emergencies
- Public health
- Legal proceedings
- State functions
- Other notified purposes

#### Biometric Data Handling

- Biometric data is **personal data** under DPDP
- **No specific "sensitive personal data" category** exists under DPDP (unlike GDPR) — all personal data is treated equally
- However, biometric data may attract **higher scrutiny** due to its immutable nature
- **Best practice**: Implement enhanced security safeguards for biometric data
- **Storage**: Consider storing biometric templates (not raw images) with encryption
- **Consent**: Explicit, informed consent required before biometric collection
- **Alternative**: Always provide a non-biometric alternative (PIN, card) for members who decline
- **Retention**: Erase biometric data when membership ends or consent is withdrawn

#### Data Retention Requirements

- Personal data must be **erased** when: purpose no longer served, consent withdrawn, or Data Principal has not engaged within retention period
- **Default retention periods** (Third Schedule to DPDP Rules): E-commerce (2 crore+ users): 3 years; Online gaming (50 lakh+ users): 3 years; Social media (2 crore+ users): 3 years
- **Gym SaaS**: No specific retention period notified; apply **purpose limitation** — erase when membership ends + reasonable grace period
- **Erasure requests**: Must be addressed within **90 days** (Rule 14)

### B5. TRAI DLT Rules for SMS

#### What DLT Registration Means

DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology) is a **blockchain-based registration system** introduced by TRAI under the **Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations (TCCCPR)**. It is **mandatory** for any business sending commercial SMS in India.

**No DLT registration = no SMS delivery.** A2P SMS not tied to a registered entity, header, and template is **scrubbed and blocked** by operators before reaching the handset.

#### Principal Entity Registration Process

1. **Register on any one operator's DLT portal** (Jio, Airtel, Vi, or BSNL)
2. Registration is **shared across all networks** — no need to register separately with each operator
3. **Documents required**: PAN of the business; GST/TAN/CIN as business proof; authorised-signatory ID; letterhead or authorisation letter; working email and mobile number for OTP verification
4. Receive a unique **Principal Entity ID (PE ID)**

#### Header (Sender ID) Registration

- **6-character sender IDs** (e.g., `MYGYM`, `VFITNS`)
- Each header is tied to a **category** with mandatory suffix (from **6 May 2025**):

| Suffix | Category | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **-P** | Promotional | Offers, discounts, marketing |
| **-S** | Service (implicit) | Account updates, alerts on existing transactions |
| **-T** | Transactional | OTPs, banking alerts |
| **-G** | Government | Government messages |

- A header registered in one category **cannot** be used for another category
- Register **separate headers** for different message types

#### Content Template Registration

- Register the **exact text** of each message with variable fields as `{#var#}`
- Every content template must contain the **brand name**
- Any **links, OTT/app links, APK links, or callback numbers** inside templates must be **pre-whitelisted** on the DLT portal
- Templates are matched in real-time; mismatches are **blocked**

#### Content Categories and Delivery Rules

| Category | Used For | Reaches DND? | Time Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transactional** | OTPs, banking alerts | Yes, all numbers, 24x7 | 24x7 |
| **Service (implicit)** | Order/delivery updates on existing transaction | Yes, all numbers, 24x7 | 24x7 |
| **Service (explicit)** | Service messages customer opted in to | Non-DND or opted-in | 24x7 |
| **Promotional** | Offers, discounts, marketing | Non-DND only | **10am to 9pm** |

#### Current TRAI Regulations 2025-2026

- **6 May 2025**: Header category suffixes (-P, -S, -T, -G) became mandatory
- **11 December 2024**: Full message traceability chain mandated
- **2025**: DLT scrubbing charge of **₹0.025 per SMS unit** for all Indian domestic commercial SMS
- **2026**: No major new regulations; focus on enforcement of existing rules

#### Practical Recommendations for Gym SaaS

1. **Register as Principal Entity** on any DLT portal (Jio/Airtel/Vi/BSNL)
2. **Register separate headers**: `GYMXXX-T` for transactional, `GYMXXX-S` for service, `GYMXXX-P` for promotional
3. **Register all content templates** with exact message text
4. **Whitelist all URLs** and callback numbers
5. **Register consent templates** for promotional opt-in
6. **Use a registered telemarketer/SMS gateway** (e.g., SmsHorizon, Exotel, Plivo, MSG91) that handles DLT compliance
7. **Send promotional SMS only between 10am-9pm** to non-DND numbers

### B6. Email Deliverability Basics

#### SPF, DKIM, DMARC Requirements

As of 2026, **all three are mandatory** for bulk senders (5,000+ messages/day to personal accounts):

| Protocol | Purpose | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **SPF** | Authorizes IP addresses to send on behalf of your domain | TXT record listing approved sending sources; **under 10 DNS lookups** |
| **DKIM** | Cryptographic signature on every outgoing message | RSA **2048-bit** keys recommended; sign the `From:` header on every message |
| **DMARC** | Instructs receivers how to handle failures | Minimum `p=none` with `rua` reporting; progress to `p=quarantine` then `p=reject` |

**Enforcement timeline:**
- **Feb 2024**: Gmail and Yahoo began enforcing bulk sender requirements (soft — 4xx deferrals)
- **May 2025**: Microsoft (Outlook/Hotmail/Live) joined enforcement
- **Nov 2025**: Gmail moved to **hard enforcement** — non-compliant messages receive **permanent 5xx SMTP rejections**

#### Bounce Rates and Sender Reputation

| Metric | Target | Critical Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard bounce rate** | < 2% | > 5% damages reputation |
| **Soft bounce rate** | < 5% | > 10% needs attention |
| **Spam complaint rate** | < 0.1% | > 0.3% triggers rejection |
| **Inbox placement rate** | > 90% | < 80% signals problems |
| **Sender Score** (Validity) | > 70 | < 70 drops placement below 50% |

**Email list degradation**: 22.5-28% annually — clean your list regularly.

**Google Postmaster Tools v2** (launched Oct 2025):
- Old v1 interface **permanently retired**
- New v2 evaluates senders on **binary Compliance Status: Pass or Fail**
- Covers: authentication, unsubscribe compliance, spam rate
- **Check weekly** — a "Fail" status risks SMTP-level rejection

#### Transactional vs Promotional Email Rules

| Aspect | Transactional | Promotional |
|---|---|---|
| **Consent required** | No (implied by transaction) | Yes (explicit opt-in) |
| **DND registry** | Reaches all numbers | Non-DND only |
| **Time window** | 24x7 | 10am-9pm (SMS rule; email has no time restriction) |
| **Unsubscribe** | Not required | Mandatory (one-click) |
| **Content** | Order confirmations, OTPs, alerts, receipts | Offers, discounts, newsletters |

**Note**: India does not have a specific email equivalent of TRAI DLT. Email regulation is primarily through **sender reputation** and **provider enforcement**.
